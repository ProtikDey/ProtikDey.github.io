Ph.D. Researcher — accessible, trustworthy, and privacy-aware GenAI

Protik Dey

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Texas at San Antonio (UTSA) working in APEX Lab led by Dr. Taslima Akter. Prior to starting my Ph.D. at UTSA, I worked three years as a Full-Stack developer at Kona Software Lab Ltd.

My research centers on making generative AI genuinely usable for blind and low-vision (BLV) people, not just technically available, but accessible, trustworthy, and privacy-aware in everyday interaction. Generative AI is increasingly woven into how people write, search, navigate, and get things done, yet these tools are rarely designed with BLV users in mind. I study how BLV people actually experience these systems: where interactions break down with assistive technology, what makes a response feel dependable or doubtful, and how people weigh the convenience of AI assistance against control over their personal information. In short, my work asks a simple question with a hard answer: how does trust actually form, and break, when BLV users rely on generative AI for everyday tasks?

I have received my B.Sc (Engg.) in Computer Science and Engineering from Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET). I completed my undergraduate thesis under the supervision of Professor Dr. Muhammad Masroor Ali on Malicious Code Detection and Analysis.

Research Projects

Trustworthy, Privacy-Aware, and Accessibile Generative AI for Blind and Low-Vision Users

This ongoing project investigates how blind and low-vision (BLV) people build, calibrate, and lose trust in generative AI tools they rely on for everyday tasks. Through interviews and study sessions with BLV participants, I examine how consistency across sessions, compatibility with assistive technology, and control over personal data shape whether these tools feel dependable. The goal is to translate these findings into concrete interaction design guidance so that generative and agentic AI systems are built with accessibility, trust, and privacy as foundations rather than afterthoughts.

Explainable AI for Blind and Low-Vision Users

As generative AI systems take on more autonomous, multi-step tasks, understanding why a system did what it did becomes critical, especially for users who cannot visually verify a system's actions. This project examines how explainability should be designed and communicated for BLV users navigating agentic AI, considering modality (how explanations are conveyed non-visually) and interpretability (what level of detail is useful versus overwhelming) as central design questions.

Financial Abuse of Older Adults in Digital Financial Systems

Financial abuse of older adults is a growing but often overlooked concern in today's increasingly digital financial landscape. Older adults face unique challenges related to access, trust, and protection, which can leave them vulnerable to exploitation. Addressing these issues is essential for safeguarding financial autonomy and dignity in later life. This line of work aims to promote more inclusive, accessible, and trustworthy financial systems by informing human-centered design and policy decisions.

Fake Bengali Facebook Post Detection on COVID-19

This project examined the spread of COVID-19 misinformation in Bengali-language Facebook posts, building a benchmark dataset collected through CrowdTangle. Using transformer-based models trained on this dataset, the study reported on how fake news correlated with audience engagement and reaction, one of the first empirical looks at Bengali-language misinformation during the pandemic.

Malicious Code Detection and Analysis

This project explored detecting malicious intent in programs before they are ever executed. Building on and extending CodeOntology, we expressed the semantic structure of Java source code as an ontology and encoded the characteristic signatures of malware such as the repeated connection calls underlying a DDoS attack — as ontology classes with formal reasoning conditions. The system successfully identified a known malware from its source code alone, produced human-interpretable explanations of why code was flagged, and remained robust against code obfuscation, where attackers scatter malicious calls across intermediate methods to evade detection.

  • Software Security
  • Static Analysis
  • Ontology
  • Semantic Web
